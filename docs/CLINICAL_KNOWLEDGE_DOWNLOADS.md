## Recommended download/import sequence

1. Keep the CDC PDFs and ADA pages as click-through references while the dentist validates the exact current content. Do not ingest the ADA chairside PDF into the commercial corpus without appropriate permission.
2. Download Open RN using the NCBI Bookshelf PDF or authorized LitArch route only if the reviewer wants the full educational textbook. Keep it in the local workstation's rights-cleared source folder, retain the CC BY 4.0 attribution/license, and index only educational excerpts. It must not be treated as a drug monograph.
3. Resolve exact product label information live through DailyMed/openFDA and preserve a record link/version. The current prototype's public lookup remains name-only; it does not send patient case details.
4. For Egypt, use EDA/WHO as jurisdictional references and manually capture exact edition/version after a pharmacist/dentist confirms the relevant product and rights. Do not reuse US-only guidance as if it were Egyptian guidance.
5. Do not use a source solely because it is free to view. Preserve source version, jurisdiction, effective/publication date, rights status, precise source section, clinical reviewer, review date and next review date.
6. Only promote a specific card out of draft after rights review, current-guideline check, a qualified dental review, and synthetic evaluation. DDI screening, dose calculation, diagnosis and prescription transmission are not implemented.

## Honest current status

The current runtime has optional RxNorm/openFDA **medicine-name metadata lookup** and the workbench now shows source-linked draft summaries for synthetic testing. The three draft evidence cards have not been clinically approved, and no interaction checker is configured. The repository contains the source directory and authored summaries/metadata, **not a bundled full copy of the Open RN book or CDC/ADA PDFs**.

In this chat's execution environment, attempts to fetch the raw PDF/archive binaries failed. The official links above remain available for a dentist to open/download directly. This avoids pretending the full source books were downloaded when only their source records and review summaries are present.
